# Semester plan and scope alignment

[Course index](../README.md) · [Assessment proposal](../assessment/README.md)

**Planning assumption:** 13 seminars of 90 minutes, matching two teaching hours per week. Confirm the actual timetable, holidays and lecturer sequence. These are seminar materials accompanying lectures, not a replacement lecture course. Definitions needed that week must have appeared in lecture or in a short seminar recap. If not, swap a nearby week or reduce the investigation rather than rushing the definition.

The official core is covered explicitly, including groups, affine geometry and 3D cross products, which a purely computational “linear algebra for AI” course can easily omit. Least squares appears only as a labeled optional replacement after orthogonality. No SVD/PCA prerequisites are required.

## Standard 90-minute pattern

| Minutes | Student work | Leader's job |
|---|---|---|
| 0–8 | Individual prediction or scheduled quiz | Collect first ideas without revealing the answer |
| 8–18 | Pair comparison and model sketch | Ask for units, domains and assumptions |
| 18–30 | Short worked start | Model one useful reasoning move, then stop |
| 30–55 | Group investigation | Listen for misconceptions; rotate roles |
| 55–70 | Changed measurement or hypothesis | Require a prediction before another computation |
| 70–82 | Proof and comparison of arguments | Connect the experiment to a definition/theorem |
| 82–90 | Individual exit or lab transfer | Get evidence of independent understanding |

The week 6 and 13 sheets provide special 30+60-minute routes. Nothing is added on top of the scheduled 90 minutes. Optional stretch problems substitute for a main activity when a group is ready; the core should remain accessible without them.

## Prerequisite checkpoints

- Before week 3, establish the group definition and matrix multiplication convention.
- Before week 5, introduce span, independence, basis and kernel. If those definitions are delayed in lecture, run tomography using “invisible changes” and explicitly supply the definitions before formal rank statements; move the abstract proof to week 6.
- Week 6's proof assumes finite-dimensional basis extension. Explain that lemma or use it as an explicitly stated result from lecture.
- Before week 9, define dot/cross products and norm. Orthogonal projection is derived in the seminar.
- Before week 12, introduce eigenvector/eigenvalue definitions so quiz 6 is retrieval, not an unseen assessment. The seminar then develops the dynamical interpretation.

Homework is due at the next seminar by default, except homework 5 should have a locally agreed deadline that allows correction before the final exam. This is a proposed cadence, not an announced deadline.
