# Five short practice and correction submissions

[Assessment guide](README.md) · [Worked examples](../solutions/practice/homework.md)

Target **20–30 minutes per task**, not a long programming assignment. Submit at most one page or a short notebook section. AI, notes and code are allowed. Include your first idea, the requested mathematics, one independent verification, and the three-sentence disclosure in the [AI policy](ai_policy.md). A no-AI route earns identical credit.

Each is worth **1 proposed point**: 0.5 for a substantive mathematical attempt with a valid verification or corrected error; 0.5 for explaining the changed case and the limit of the original argument. Give brief feedback and one chance to correct a misconception. Do not grade prose polish or prompt length.

1. **After week 2 — balance certificate.** Invent a three-node directed cycle with nonzero external balances summing to zero. Give a family of flows and its nonnegative range. Change one balance so the system is inconsistent, and certify the contradiction without a new elimination.
2. **After week 4 — audit an LU claim.** Critique: “If elimination reaches a zero pivot, the matrix is singular and no LU factorization exists.” Give one invertible matrix that needs a row swap and one singular matrix that nevertheless has unit-lower LU. Verify both products and explain the distinction.
3. **After week 7 — same samples, different object.** Construct two distinct quadratic polynomials with the same values at 0 and 1. Derive the kernel of the evaluation map and design one additional evaluation that makes reconstruction unique for every quadratic. State the bad choices of the third sampling point.
4. **After week 10 — coordinates are not the object.** Choose an invertible nonorthogonal $2\times2$ basis matrix $S$ and a non-scalar linear map $A$. Compute $S^{-1}AS$. Verify $ASc=SCc$ for a chosen $c$ and explain why testing only one $c$ is not a proof. Prove the identity for all $c$. Find whether the wrong order $SAS^{-1}$ fails on your example.
5. **After week 12 — convergence claim.** Critique: “A stochastic matrix always settles to a unique stationary distribution.” Use the two-state family $P(a,b)$, classify the boundary cases, and choose one convergent example with a negative second eigenvalue. Predict whether the approach alternates and verify two iterations.
