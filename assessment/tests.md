# Public practice tests — two A/B pairs

[Assessment guide](README.md) · [Worked marking guides](../solutions/practice/tests.md)

Each form is **30 minutes, 10 points**. Show reasoning; no AI or calculator is needed. Times are suggested, not separate cutoffs. These forms are public rehearsal resources and have not been calibrated for equal difficulty.

## Test 1 — week 6; material through week 5

### Form A

1. **3 points, 8 minutes.** Classify every real parameter case for $x+y=3$, $2x+ay=b$. Give all solutions where they exist and justify the exceptional case.
2. **2 points, 5 minutes.** Let $R=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$ and $F=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$. Compute $RF e_1$ and $FR e_1$. State and verify the correct inverse formula for $RF$.
3. **3 points, 9 minutes.** Four image measurements give the complete family $(p,q,r,s)=(t,5-t,6-t,3+t)$. Find the nonnegative parameter interval. Decide whether measuring total brightness removes ambiguity. Reconstruct if the extra measurement is $p+s=7$, and justify uniqueness.
4. **2 points, 8 minutes.** Refute “every singular matrix fails to have LU” by giving a nonzero singular $2\times2$ matrix and a unit-lower factorization. Explain why this does not imply a unique solution for every right-hand side.

### Form B

1. **3 points, 8 minutes.** Classify every real parameter case for $2x+y=4$, $4x+ay=b$. Give all solutions where they exist and justify the exceptional case.
2. **2 points, 5 minutes.** Let $M=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$ and $D=\operatorname{diag}(2,1)$. Compute $MD e_2$ and $DM e_2$. State and verify the correct inverse formula for $MD$.
3. **3 points, 9 minutes.** Four image measurements give the complete family $(p,q,r,s)=(t,7-t,8-t,t-1)$. Find the nonnegative parameter interval. Decide whether measuring total brightness removes ambiguity. Reconstruct if $p+s=7$, and justify uniqueness.
4. **2 points, 8 minutes.** Refute “LU implies invertibility” with a nonzero singular $2\times2$ matrix, a unit-lower factorization and a right-hand side for which the solution is not unique.

## Test 2 — week 13; material through week 12

### Form A

1. **3 points, 8 minutes.** For $T:\mathbb R^3\to\mathbb R^2$ with matrix $\begin{pmatrix}1&0&1\\0&1&1\end{pmatrix}$, find a kernel basis, rank, and whether $T$ is injective or onto. Justify each conclusion.
2. **2 points, 6 minutes.** For $A=\operatorname{diag}(2,1)$ and new basis matrix $S=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$, derive the new coordinate matrix and check it on $e_2$ in the new coordinates.
3. **2 points, 6 minutes.** Project $b=(3,1)^T$ orthogonally onto span$((1,2)^T)$. Give the residual and explain why perpendicularity certifies a closest point.
4. **3 points, 10 minutes.** For $P=\begin{pmatrix}0.8&0.3\\0.2&0.7\end{pmatrix}$, find a stationary probability vector and both eigenvalues. Explain convergence of all initial probability vectors. Give a stochastic matrix where nonstationary initial vectors do not converge.

### Form B

1. **3 points, 8 minutes.** For $T:\mathbb R^3\to\mathbb R^2$ with matrix $\begin{pmatrix}1&1&0\\0&1&1\end{pmatrix}$, find a kernel basis, rank, and whether $T$ is injective or onto. Justify each conclusion.
2. **2 points, 6 minutes.** For $A=\operatorname{diag}(1,3)$ and new basis matrix $S=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1&1\end{pmatrix}$, derive the new coordinate matrix and check it on $e_1$ in the new coordinates.
3. **2 points, 6 minutes.** Project $b=(2,0)^T$ orthogonally onto span$((1,-1)^T)$. Give the residual and explain why perpendicularity certifies a closest point.
4. **3 points, 10 minutes.** For $P=\begin{pmatrix}0.7&0.1\\0.3&0.9\end{pmatrix}$, find a stationary probability vector and both eigenvalues. Explain convergence of all initial probability vectors. Give a stochastic matrix with more than one stationary probability vector.
