# Proposed task-level AI policy

[Assessment guide](README.md)

AI can produce convincing answers, including incorrect ones. The learning goal is to formulate a model, understand its hypotheses, check a result, and explain what changes when an assumption changes. No task is advertised as “AI-proof.”

| Task | Permitted tools | What the student must demonstrate |
|---|---|---|
| Initial individual prediction | Paper or approved accessible equivalent; no AI | A committed first idea, even if wrong |
| Group investigation and practice homework | Notes, code, symbolic tools, AI if permitted for that session | A checkable model, one independently verified claim, and a limitation or changed case |
| Quizzes, tests, individual exit/transfer | No AI or communication tools; only explicitly announced aids/accommodations | Independent explanation or calculation |
| Final exam | Lecturer's published rules | Do not infer permissions from practice rules |

## Short disclosure for AI-assisted practice

Write three sentences: **what help you used**, **which claim you checked and how**, and **what remains uncertain**. If you used no AI, say so and describe your check anyway. A full chat transcript is neither required nor desirable. Students may use an instructor-supplied flawed solution instead of an AI service, with identical credit available.

Useful verification includes substituting into every equation, checking dimensions and units, constructing a counterexample, treating zero/degenerate cases, or proving a claim from definitions. Asking a second model is not by itself independent verification. Numerical agreement is evidence for an example, not proof of a universal statement.

Do not upload classmates' work, personal data, grades, private assessments, or copyrighted course material to external services without authorization. Do not require a paid account. Provide paper, text-table and local-code routes. Never infer misconduct from writing style or an AI detector score; use the institution's process and discuss specific evidence with the student. The supervised transfer tasks assess learning regardless of how polished the submitted artifact looks.

## Five reusable critique prompts

1. Find a step that divides by a quantity that might be zero; classify the missing case.
2. Separate a basis from the space it spans; state the ambient dimension.
3. Name the hypothesis used when reversing an implication.
4. Construct the smallest counterexample you can and verify it exactly.
5. Change one measurement, coordinate basis, or model assumption; predict the effect before computing.
